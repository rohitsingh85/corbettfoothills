## The Taste of the Hills

Kumaoni cuisine is one of India's best-kept culinary secrets. Unlike the rich, cream-based gravies of the plains or the fiery curries of the east, the food of the Kumaon Himalayas is defined by simplicity, seasonality, and a deep respect for ingredients. At Corbett Foothills Retreat, we have built our dining philosophy around these principles — serving food that is honest, nourishing, and deeply connected to the land.

## The Kumaoni Pantry

The traditional Kumaoni kitchen relies on a handful of staple ingredients, each chosen for its ability to thrive in the mountain climate:

- **Mandua (finger millet)** — A nutrient-dense grain that forms the base of traditional rotis and porridges.
- **Gahat (horse gram)** — A protein-rich legume used in soups, curries, and the famous *gahat ka shorba*.
- **Bhatt (black soybean)** — Slow-cooked with spices to create a rich, earthy curry.
- **Jhangora (barnyard millet)** — A light, easily digestible grain often cooked as a savoury rice substitute.
- **Seasonal greens** — Wild and cultivated varieties including *linguda* (fiddlehead ferns), *chulai* (amaranth), and *bathua* (lamb's quarters).

## Signature Dishes

### Bhatt ki Churdkani

A quintessential Kumaoni preparation, this slow-cooked black soybean curry achieves its depth of flavour through patient simmering — often for three to four hours over a wood fire. The beans are tempered with jakhiya (a local wild spice similar to mustard seed), garlic, and dried red chillies. The result is a dish that is simultaneously rustic and sophisticated.

### Gahat ka Shorba

A nourishing soup made from horse gram, traditionally consumed during the cold winter months to ward off illness. The soup is lightly spiced with cumin, ginger, and turmeric, and finished with a squeeze of lemon. At the retreat, we serve it as a welcome drink — a warm introduction to the flavours of Kumaon.

### Aloo ke Gutke

The quintessential Kumaoni comfort food. Boiled potatoes are roughly crushed and tempered with jakhiya, red chilli, and turmeric. Simple, humble, and utterly satisfying — especially when eaten with a fresh mandua roti and a dollop of homemade ghee.

### Singhori

A traditional sweet made from khoya (reduced milk) wrapped in a maul (madar) leaf and steamed. The leaf imparts a delicate, earthy aroma that distinguishes this dessert from any other Indian sweet. It is a rare delicacy, one that our kitchen team prepares with skill passed down through generations.

## Our Organic Kitchen Garden

At Corbett Foothills Retreat, the journey from farm to fork is measured in metres, not kilometres. Our kitchen garden supplies a significant portion of the produce used in daily meal preparation. Guests are invited to walk through the garden, pick their own vegetables, and even participate in the cooking process.

What grows in our garden changes with the seasons:

- **Winter (Nov–Feb):** Spinach, mustard greens, carrots, radish, peas, cauliflower.
- **Spring (Mar–Apr):** Tomatoes, brinjal (aubergine), chillies, coriander, mint.
- **Summer (May–Jun):** Papaya, mango, bottle gourd, ridge gourd, okra.
- **Monsoon (Jul–Sep):** Turmeric, ginger, sweet potato, yam.

## The Dining Experience

Meals at Corbett Foothills Retreat are served in a communal dining space overlooking the forest edge. There are no set menus — the kitchen prepares what the garden and local markets have provided that day. This approach requires flexibility from our guests, but it rewards them with food that is as fresh and flavourful as any they will ever taste.

For guests who prefer privacy, in-suite dining is always available. Our team will set up a private dinner on your veranda, surrounded by the sounds of the forest and the flickering light of oil lamps.

## Cooking Classes

For those who wish to take a piece of Kumaon home with them, we offer informal cooking classes with our kitchen team. Learn to make Bhatt ki Churdkani, master the technique of mandua roti, or simply spend an hour learning about the spices and ingredients that define this remarkable culinary tradition.
