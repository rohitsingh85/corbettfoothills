## A Century of Change

The forests of Kumaon have witnessed a remarkable transformation over the past century. Once hunted to the brink of extinction under colonial rule, the Bengal tiger — *Panthera tigris tigris* — has staged one of the most celebrated recoveries in the history of wildlife conservation. Nowhere is this more evident than in the landscapes surrounding Jim Corbett National Park, where tiger populations have rebounded to become one of the densest in India.

## The Corbett Legacy

The park itself is named after Jim Corbett, the legendary hunter-turned-conservationist who famously tracked and killed man-eating tigers in these very forests in the early 1900s. Ironically, Corbett would later become one of the most vocal advocates for tiger protection, helping establish India's first national park here in 1936. His evolution from hunter to hero mirrors the broader shift in human attitudes toward the tiger — from adversary to icon.

## Project Tiger and Its Impact

When Project Tiger launched in 1973, the Corbett landscape was one of nine original reserves selected for protection. At the time, India's tiger population had plummeted to fewer than 2,000 individuals. Today, thanks to decades of dedicated conservation work, the Corbett Tiger Reserve alone is home to over 250 tigers — one of the highest densities in any protected area in the world.

Key factors driving this recovery include:

- **Strict anti-poaching measures**, including camera traps, drone surveillance, and dedicated forest patrols.
- **Community engagement programs** that provide alternative livelihoods to reduce dependence on forest resources.
- **Scientific corridor management** ensuring genetic connectivity between fragmented habitats.
- **Eco-tourism revenue sharing** that incentivises local communities to protect rather than exploit wildlife.

## The Ripple Effect

The return of the tiger has had a cascading positive effect on the entire ecosystem. With the apex predator restored, prey species populations have stabilised, forest undergrowth has regenerated, and the overall biodiversity of the region has flourished. Bird species, in particular, have benefited from the protected habitat — over 600 species are now recorded in the Corbett landscape.

## Challenges Ahead

Despite the successes, challenges remain. Human-wildlife conflict continues to rise as development encroaches on traditional tiger corridors. Climate change threatens to alter the composition of the sal forests that form the backbone of tiger habitat. And the balancing act between tourism revenue and ecological carrying capacity requires constant vigilance.
