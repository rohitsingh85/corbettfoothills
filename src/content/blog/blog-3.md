## A Birder's Paradise

Jim Corbett National Park is one of India's premier birding destinations, with over 600 recorded species spanning resident populations and winter migrants. The park's remarkable habitat diversity — from riverine belts and grasslands to dense sal forests and rocky escarpments — supports an extraordinary avian richness that rivals any protected area in South Asia.

## When to Visit

| Season | Months | Highlights |
|--------|--------|------------|
| Winter | Nov–Feb | Migratory waterfowl, thrushes, warblers. Best for species diversity. |
| Spring | Mar–Apr | Breeding plumage, raptor migration, resident breeding activity. |
| Summer | May–Jun | Bird activity near water sources, cuckoo calls, monsoon arrivals. |
| Monsoon | Jul–Sep | Park closed. Good for surrounding forest birding. |

## The Essential Checklist

### Raptors and Birds of Prey

- **Crested Serpent Eagle** — Common year-round. Look for them perched on sal branches along the Ramganga river.
- **Changeable Hawk-Eagle** — Often seen soaring above the Dhikala zone grasslands.
- **Pallas's Fish Eagle** — Winter visitor. Scan the Kosi riverbanks near the retreat.
- **Black Eagle** — Distinctive silhouette with broad wings, often over dense forest canopy.
- **Eurasian Sparrowhawk** — Shy forest raptor, best spotted during early morning drives.

### Hornbills and Barbets

- **Great Hornbill** — The flagship bird of Corbett. Best sighted in the Bijrani and Jhirna zones during summer.
- **Oriental Pied Hornbill** — More common and widespread than its larger cousin. Listen for its cackling call.
- **Coppersmith Barbet** — The rhythmic *tuk-tuk-tuk* call is one of the sounds of the retreat garden.
- **Lineated Barbet** — Larger than the Coppersmith, with a distinctive white-streaked head.

### Kingfishers

- **Common Kingfisher** — Found along every stream and water body in the park.
- **White-throated Kingfisher** — The most conspicuous kingfisher, often far from water.
- **Pied Kingfisher** — Hover-hunter over the Ramganga reservoir.
- **Stork-billed Kingfisher** — A rare treat. Deep orange bill and turquoise back.

### Woodpeckers

- **Greater Flameback** — One of the most striking woodpeckers in the region.
- **Lesser Yellownape** — Often in mixed foraging flocks.
- **Rufous Woodpecker** — Uncommon, associated with ant nests in the forest.
- **Heart-spotted Woodpecker** — A special sighting for any birder's life list.

### Waterfowl and Waders

- **Sarus Crane** — The world's tallest flying bird, seen in agricultural fields near the park.
- **Lesser Adjutant Stork** — Threatened species, found near wetlands.
- **River Lapwing** — Along every gravel riverbank.
- **Common Snipe** — Well-camouflaged winter visitor in marshy areas.

## Tips for the Corbett Birder

1. **Start early.** Bird activity peaks between 5:30 AM and 9:00 AM. The retreat can arrange packed breakfasts for early safari departures.
2. **Listen first, look second.** Many of Corbett's most exciting species are heard long before they are seen. Learn the calls of the Great Hornbill, the Crested Serpent Eagle, and the Oriental Scops Owl before your trip.
3. **Carry a scope.** While binoculars are essential, a spotting scope is invaluable for scanning the Ramganga river for waterfowl and the distant treetops for raptors.
4. **Dress appropriately.** Neutral colours, layered clothing, and sturdy walking shoes. Avoid synthetics that rustle — the jungle amplifies every sound.
5. **Patience is everything.** The best sightings come to those who wait. Find a promising spot, sit quietly, and let the forest reveal itself.

## Beyond the Park Boundaries

Some of the best birding in the Corbett landscape happens outside the park. The buffer zone, village woodlands, and even the grounds of Corbett Foothills Retreat itself host a remarkable diversity of species. Keep an eye on the fruiting fig trees near the retreat dining area — they attract barbets, bulbuls, and the occasional hornbill. Early morning walks along the Kosi riverbed, before the safari traffic begins, can yield sightings of Pallas's Fish Eagle, River Lapwing, and the elusive Ibisbill.
