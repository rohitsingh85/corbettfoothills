## Legends the Hills Still Whisper

The Kumaon region has been inhabited for thousands of years, and its oral traditions carry stories that predate written history. Every village, every forest patch, and every stream seems to have a tale attached to it — some romantic, some cautionary, and some that send a chill down your spine even in the warmth of the afternoon sun.

**The Legend of the Naga Kings** — Deep within the forests of Kumaon, local communities speak of the Nagas, serpent beings who are said to dwell in hidden underground chambers beneath the hills. These beings are not malevolent in the traditional sense, but they are fiercely protective of their territory. Villagers in remote parts of the Kumaon foothills still leave offerings of milk and rice at certain old trees and rock formations, believing that disturbing the Nagas brings misfortune to those who venture too deep into the forest without respect. Temples dedicated to Naga deities can be found across the region, their quiet presence a reminder that the hills hold secrets we may never fully understand.

**The Story of the Gollu Devta** — In the hills around Ramnagar and the Corbett landscape, many families worship Gollu Devta, a local deity believed to protect travellers and forest dwellers from harm. Legend has it that Gollu Devta was once a human who disappeared into the forest under mysterious circumstances and later began appearing to villagers in dreams, offering guidance and warnings. Even today, it is common for locals to whisper a quick prayer to Gollu Devta before entering dense forest areas, especially after dark. The deity is said to manifest as a flickering light among the trees, leading lost travellers back to safety.

![Ancient banyan tree with roots and vines in Corbett forest — rumoured to be a dwelling place of spirits](/images/mysterious-kumaon-3.webp)

**The Witch of the Kumaon Hills (Dakan)** — One of the most persistent figures in Kumaoni folklore is the Dakan, a witch-like spirit said to roam the forests and village outskirts during the night. Unlike the cartoonish witches of Western stories, the Dakan of Kumaon is a deeply unsettling figure — described as an old woman with feet turned backwards, who lures unsuspecting travellers deep into the forest before vanishing without a trace. Parents in Kumaoni villages have used stories of the Dakan for generations to keep children from wandering into the forest alone at night. Many adults will admit, perhaps with a nervous laugh, that they still feel a quickening of the pulse when walking past a dark patch of forest after sunset.

## Haunted Places of Kumaon

For travellers intrigued by the paranormal, Kumaon offers several locations that have developed reputations for unusual activity. These are not amusement park haunted houses — they are real places, often surrounded by forest, where locals and visitors alike have reported experiences they struggle to explain.

**The Abandoned Bungalow at Mohan** — Hidden deep within the Corbett reserve, near the edge of the forest where the Kosi river meets the woodland, stands an old abandoned bungalow from the British era. Locals in the nearby villages avoid this structure, especially after dark. Stories speak of colonial-era residents who died under mysterious circumstances, and subsequent visitors have reported hearing footsteps on the upper floor, the sound of a woman humming an old English tune, and the sensation of being watched from the dark windows. Forest officials who patrol the area rarely stay there overnight, preferring to complete their rounds and return to their quarters before sunset.

![British-era abandoned bungalow in Corbett forest with overgrown vegetation](/images/mysterious-kumaon-5.webp)

**The Forest Rest House at Dhikala** — One of the most famous — or infamous — forest rest houses in India, the Dhikala Forest Rest House has been the subject of countless stories shared by wildlife enthusiasts, photographers, and forest staff over the decades. Built during the British period, this sprawling rest house sits in the heart of the Corbett Tiger Reserve, surrounded by vast grasslands and dense forest. Many visitors have reported strange occurrences: doors opening on their own, the sound of someone walking on the veranda when no one is there, and an inexplicable feeling of being watched during the night. Some say the spirits of British officers who once managed the forest still patrol the corridors. Others believe the rest house is simply too close to the wild, and the boundary between the human world and the forest world blurs here more easily than elsewhere.

**The Old Cemetery at Garjiya** — Near the famous Garjiya Devi Temple, perched on a rock in the middle of the Kosi River, lies an old cemetery that dates back to the British era. The graves are overgrown, the inscriptions weathered and barely legible. Local legend says that on certain nights — particularly during the full moon — a woman in white can be seen walking among the graves before disappearing into the forest. While no definitive explanation exists, the cemetery remains a place that locals prefer not to visit after dark.

## Ghostly Tales of Forest Rest Houses

The forest rest houses scattered across Kumaon's wildlife reserves are some of the most atmospherically charged places you could ever spend a night. Built over a century ago, these structures have witnessed countless seasons, forest fires, wildlife encounters, and the comings and goings of forest officers, hunters, and naturalists from a bygone era. Their thick stone walls, high ceilings, and creaking wooden floors hold memories that some believe never truly fade.

**Rest House No. 2 at Kalagarh** — Located on the western boundary of the Corbett reserve, this rest house has a reputation that precedes it among forest staff. Multiple guards and rangers have reported seeing the figure of a tall man in a safari suit standing on the lawn at dusk, only to find the spot empty when they look again. A former forest officer who stayed there in the 1990s wrote in the visitor log about being woken up by the sound of footsteps circling his bed, though the room was empty and the door was locked from the inside. The logbook entries from that period, if you can access them, make for chilling reading.

**The Baraipani Forest Rest House** — Perched at the edge of a steep valley, this rest house is surrounded by dense oak and rhododendron forest that traps the mist and creates an atmosphere of complete isolation. Guests have reported hearing the sound of women laughing somewhere in the forest at night, though the nearest village is several kilometres away. Others describe vivid dreams that feel more real than ordinary sleep, and a persistent feeling that someone is standing right behind them when they look out of the windows at the dark tree line.

## The Spirits of the River and Forest

Kumaon's natural features — its rivers, cliffs, and dense forests — are not merely geographical landmarks in the local worldview. They are living entities, inhabited by spirits that demand respect and acknowledgment.

**The Kosi River's Benevolent Spirit** — The Kosi River, which flows through the Corbett landscape and past the Garjiya Devi Temple, is believed by locals to be protected by a benevolent female spirit known as Kosi Mata. Fishermen and villagers who live along its banks offer prayers before entering the water, especially during the monsoon season when the river swells with dangerous force. There are stories of people who were caught in the river's current and rescued by an unseen hand, which locals attribute to Kosi Mata. Even today, you will find small shrines along the riverbank where marigold flowers and incense are offered each morning.

**The Forest Dwellers — Churails and Brahmarakshas** — In the deeper forests of Kumaon, particularly in areas that have remained undisturbed for generations, locals speak of Churails (malevolent spirits of women who died under tragic circumstances) and Brahmarakshas (the restless spirits of priests or holy men who met violent ends). These beings are said to inhabit specific trees, caves, or clearings within the forest. Villagers who need to pass through these areas carry specific herbs, recite protective mantras, or simply avoid the locations altogether after dark. While modern visitors may dismiss these as superstitions, the consistency and detail of these accounts across different villages and generations suggest a cultural memory that runs very deep.

## Unexplained Encounters in the Corbett Landscape

Beyond the traditional folklore, there are modern accounts from travellers, naturalists, and forest staff that add a contemporary layer to Kumaon's mysterious reputation.

**The Disappearing Fire** — Several trekkers and wildlife photographers have reported an odd phenomenon in the forests near Ramnagar: a flickering orange light that appears at a distance during the night, seeming to move between trees, leading those who follow it deeper into the forest before vanishing abruptly. Forest department staff have a name for this — they call it "the wandering light" — and explicitly warn visitors not to follow it. Some attribute it to natural gas emissions or bioluminescent fungi, but those who have seen it describe it as behaving too intentionally to be a natural phenomenon.

**Voices in the Wind** — The Dhikala zone, considered the heart of Corbett, is known for its vast open grasslands surrounded by forest. Several visitors have reported hearing what sounds like distant conversations carried on the wind, particularly in the late evening when the forest settles into nighttime silence. The voices are indistinct, never clear enough to make out words, but the tone and cadence are unmistakably human. Forest guards who have spent decades in the zone simply smile when asked about it. "The forest speaks," one of them once said. "You just have to be quiet enough to hear it."
