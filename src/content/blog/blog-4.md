## Hospitality at the Forest Edge

Running a retreat at the edge of a tiger reserve presents unique challenges that no hotel management textbook can prepare you for. At Corbett Foothills Retreat, our six suites are separated from one of the world's most famous national parks by just 8 kilometres of forest. This proximity to wilderness is our greatest asset — and our most demanding responsibility.

## The Morning Routine

Our day begins at 4:30 AM, long before our guests stir. The kitchen team fires up the stoves to prepare packed breakfasts for early safari departures. The gardening team inspects the perimeter for any animal activity overnight — a routine that becomes second nature when your neighbours include leopards, elephants, and the occasional sloth bear.

The housekeeping team works in near silence, mindful that many guests are birdwatchers who rise early. Windows are opened to let in the morning air — not just for freshness, but so guests wake to the sounds of the forest.

## The Kitchen Philosophy

Our kitchen operates on a farm-to-table model that most urban restaurants can only aspire to. Vegetables are harvested each morning from our organic garden — the menu changes not by week or by day, but by the hour, depending on what is ripe enough to pick.

This approach creates unique challenges. A sudden hailstorm can wipe out a week's worth of salad greens. A troop of langurs can strip a papaya tree overnight. But it also creates extraordinary moments — the taste of a sun-warmed tomato still carrying the heat of the soil, or a Kumaoni dal made with lentils grown in our own fields.

## Managing Wildlife Encounters

One of the most frequently asked questions from potential guests is: *Is it safe?* The answer requires nuance. Corbett Foothills Retreat is safe because we take safety seriously. Our staff are trained in wildlife conflict avoidance. All guest areas are well-lit at night. Garbage is securely stored to avoid attracting animals. And a simple set of guidelines — never walk alone after dark, keep doors closed, do not feed the monkeys — keeps both guests and wildlife safe.

The real secret is understanding animal behaviour. Our naturalists can read the forest in ways that sophisticated technology cannot match. A sudden silence from the birds means a predator is near. Alarm calls from langurs pinpoint the location and species of a threat. This ancient knowledge, passed down through generations of forest dwellers, is the foundation of our safety protocol.

## The Art of Anticipation

With only six suites, personalisation is not a goal — it is a given. Before each guest's arrival, we learn their preferences: do they prefer a firm or soft pillow? Are they early risers or night owls? Do they have dietary restrictions? Do they want an adventurous safari itinerary or a quiet retreat?

This level of attention is only possible because of our scale. Unlike large resorts where guests are processed through a standardised system, Corbett Foothills Retreat treats each stay as a unique relationship. Our staff-to-guest ratio is nearly one-to-one, allowing us to anticipate needs before they are expressed.

## The Quiet Hours

When the last guest has retired for the night and the forest settles into its nocturnal rhythm, a different world emerges. Civets visit the garden. Owls call from the roofline. The distant alarm of a sambar deer echoes through the dark. For the staff, these quiet hours are a time for reflection, for planning the next day, and for appreciating the extraordinary privilege of living and working at the edge of the wild.
